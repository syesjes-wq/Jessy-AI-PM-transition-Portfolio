# Jobs to Be Done

This document defines the core motivation behind HabitlyKids using the Jobs to Be Done framework — describing the situation, the desired progress, and how success is measured.

---

## The Job

**When** my kids' daily routines have become a constant struggle — repeated reminders, resistance, and me getting stressed or losing my temper —

**I want** a way for my children to practice and own their daily habits,

**So that** good habits form over time, without me having to push, nag, or raise my voice to make it happen.

---

## How it works

Children earn stars for completing their daily tasks. The reward mechanic gives them a reason to show up — the habit forms in the background as a byproduct of consistent repetition.

From the child's perspective: complete your tasks, earn stars, work toward something you want.

From the parent's perspective: the kids are practising their habits, the system holds the accountability, and you are not the one doing the enforcing.

---

## What progress looks like

The child starts and completes their routine independently — not because they were asked, but because they have their own motivation to do it.

---

## What validated this

Running a manual star chart at home for two weeks with two boys, aged 4 and 6, produced exactly that. On day 14, both boys woke up, got dressed, and were ready for school at 6am — before I came back from the gym. No reminders. No asking. They had calculated the night before how many stars they needed and decided to get a head start.

That morning is the benchmark every product decision is tested against.

> *"Would this feature create that 6am morning? If yes — build it. If not — deprioritize it."*
